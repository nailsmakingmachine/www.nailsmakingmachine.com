Quick Specification Overview

An automatic wire nail making machine is an industrial mechanical system that converts drawn steel wire into finished collated, common, or specialty nails using synchronized feeding, cutting, heading, and ejection cycles. Modern systems achieve continuous production rates between 450 and 800 pieces per minute, handling wire diameters from 1.6 mm to 6.0 mm with dimensional head tolerances within ±0.05 mm.

Mechanical Operation & Production Stages

  1. Straightening & Wire De-coiling: High-tensile steel wire is drawn continuously through five-roller bi-axial straightening blocks to remove coil cast and curvature.
  2. Cold-Heading Stage: Reciprocating punch dies compress the wire end against clamping jaws under 15–30 metric tons of force to form the nail head with uniform concentricity.
  3. Tip Pointing & Shearing: Tungsten-carbide cutter dies pinch and sever the wire, shaping standard diamond points while leaving waste whiskers under 0.02 grams per unit.
  4. Centrifugal Ejection: Mechanical kickers divert finished nails into sorting chutes for subsequent tumbling, de-burring, or electro-galvanizing.

Frequently Asked Engineering Questions

What wire gauge is ideal for common round nails?

Most common construction nails (2" to 3.5") use 9 to 11 gauge (3.0 mm to 3.7 mm) low-carbon drawn wire with tensile ratings calibrated between 600 and 750 N/mm² to prevent buckling under punch impact.

How frequently must carbide cutters be sharpened?

Standard tungsten-carbide cutting dies run 120 to 160 operational hours (equivalent to approximately 4 to 6 million nails) between surface grinds under continuous lubricant flooding.
